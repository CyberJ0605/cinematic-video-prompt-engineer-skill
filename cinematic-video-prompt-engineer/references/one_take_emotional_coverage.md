# Emotional Coverage, Facing and Contact in a True One-Take

Use for emotion-led true continuous shots with near/far attention, an approach, critical hands/props, or repairs of those controls. Apply the evidence section to generated-result reviews. This is a targeted audit, not a requirement that every shot start with hands, zoom, orbit or contain close-ups.

## Generated-result evidence and attribution

- Identify the actual media and available prompt. Inspect metadata and representative frames; sample the failed action more densely. State timestamps and inspection limits. Do not claim full-speed playback, audio verification, frame-accurate continuity or exact optics from sampled stills. If the actual submitted prompt/settings are unavailable, identify the conversation draft being compared rather than assuming it was submitted unchanged.
- Separate `observed symptom -> controlling text -> likely cause -> smallest repair`. Distinguish missing/conflicting direction, clear instructions not followed, ambiguous evidence, and unknown generation/export settings. A missed explicit instruction is an execution deviation in this sample, not proof the model is incapable. Do not invent responsibility percentages.
- Report visible media specifications separately from causes: unexpected aspect ratio can result from generation settings, cropping or export. Do not identify or blame a model solely from filename/dimensions.
- Preserve successful story, identity, setting, light and ending. Group repairs around up to three dominant controls; disclose necessary choreography changes. Changing several variables can be useful for production revision but is not a single-variable causal test.
- Small, defocused or occluded contact supports `contact/support is unreadable`, not a claim about a specific fused finger or anatomical defect. Increase evidence sampling rather than inventing confidence.
- Text regression establishes prompt consistency only. For causal testing, propose matched settings/input with one control family changed at a time and repeated samples if supported. Do not generate or spend credits without authorization, or claim the repair worked before reviewing new media.

## Emotional evidence must have usable coverage

Internally connect each important emotional beat to its visible proof, frame scale, focus target and readable dwell: tension -> thumb presses photo edge -> hand detail; recognition -> directed gaze and failed start -> face close view; allowing help -> load transfer -> readable hand contact; release -> breath and returning gaze -> held face.

- Emotion words do not help if the face is outside the frame, too small, turned away or blurred during the beat. A waist crop containing a tiny photo does not guarantee emotional hand detail.
- Include a hand/face close view when it carries the scene and the brief permits. Otherwise use readable medium/full-frame behavior. Do not mandate close-ups in an explicitly locked wide shot.
- Reserve time to settle on evidence. Passing a face is not automatically performance coverage. Protect reaction time after the decisive word and 1–2 seconds of ending consequence.
- A true one-take means continuous time and camera path, not one shot size. Design continuous coverage phases rather than independent close-ups disguised as a single shot.

## World-space facing, gaze and screen position

- Lock target and fixed landmark first: father beside bin, daughter several metres away along the platform, clear connecting path. Screen-left/right describes projection, not body orientation.
- For a critical look, specify necessary feet/torso orientation, head turn and gaze landing. The body may remain oblique while the head turns naturally. Do not demand an impossible neck twist for a flattering face angle.
- Choose a camera side that can read the gaze. A woman looking straight away into deep background cannot simultaneously present a frontal face to the camera behind her. Use a credible three-quarter/profile view or continuous lateral reposition.
- Preserve the evidence chain: target locatable -> gaze acquires target -> hesitation -> call -> listener hears and turns. If the listener notices only after the call, do not give anticipatory eye contact.
- During an arc, screen coordinates/background projections may change; world landmarks and actual positions do not. Never lock someone facing screen-left throughout a camera orbit.

## Continuous camera and time budget

For each required phase check `start scale/position -> actual move or lens change -> end scale/position -> evidence dwell`. Print only useful controls.

- `Rack Focus` changes the sharp plane, not camera location or subject size. `Zoom` changes field of view from the same location. Zoom back out before a full-body approach if needed. Do not call a focus pull a zoom.
- `Dolly` changes camera position/perspective; `Tilt` changes view direction; `Pedestal/Crane` changes height. A backward/upward reveal may follow one gentle diagonal physical path with compatible reframing.
- For an arc, specify start side, end side, limited extent and open route. Full orbit is not required. The face may leave view for spatial clarification, but not throughout its important emotional beat.
- Do not lock a prime lens and later demand optical zoom. Numeric lens settings are optional intent, not exact optical simulation guarantees.
- Budget real walking and camera travel. Near/far reactions must not teleport camera or actor. Restore a readable wide view before the first walking step and retain the whole approach. Zoom attention without relocating camera, then follow via a physically continuous route.
- In a dense 30-second take, protect story evidence, approach/contact and reactions before decorative arcs. If several phases are requested, serialize with landing frames; neither stack all moves simultaneously nor reject them under an automatic one-move quota.
- No hard cut, masked cut, full-frame foreground wipe or black transition in a requested true one-take. Partial natural occlusion cannot hide a reset or a specifically required visible action.

## Shared-object contact and background workload

For two people lifting, handing over or supporting one object, identify `initial support -> distinct grip/role per person -> force/pivot -> stable end support -> release order`.

- Avoid indistinguishable hands on one small grip. Separate contact regions and preserve who bears the load.
- If exact teamwork is not story-critical, simplify without losing meaning: one person rights a manageable bin while the cleaner clears the lid, then takes over once its base is stable. Disclose this change. If simultaneous two-person lifting is explicitly required, retain it with distinct grips and readable framing.
- Show enough object and supporting bodies to understand force. Depth of field may subordinate the action but must not erase necessary contact evidence. Stage the main lift after the opening detail reveals it, not entirely in blur or occlusion.
- Story-carrying background action is not generic ambience: give it a short defined state change and completion cue. Other travelers/workers keep independent low-intensity activity without occupying the principal approach/contact corridor at the decisive moment.
- For a bag handoff, the giver supports until the receiver visibly closes a grip and takes the weight; only then release. Preserve photo/bag handedness and final ownership throughout departure.

## Final targeted check

Can the viewer read emotion, locate the gaze target, follow the actual approach, understand contact and see the changed relationship? Are camera phases compatible/timed without hidden resets? Are locked-camera, single-variable and no-reference requests intact? Record text checks separately from generated-video review.
