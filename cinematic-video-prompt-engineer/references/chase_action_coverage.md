# Chase Causality and Genre-Matched Action Coverage

Use the pursuit controls for chase, escape, interception and obstacle-driven distance changes. Use the coverage section for action cinematography when relevant. These are conditional extensions of the local state audit and continuity contract, not mandatory prompt fields, shot counts or extra events.

## Goal, motion and distance

- Give pursuer and target distinct present goals: acquire/reach the target versus reach an escape route/avoid capture. Choose visible behavior that expresses them; running in the same direction alone does not establish pursuit. Do not require every chase to include looking back, crowds or obstacles.
- Treat the gap as a changing relationship: lead -> visible cause -> larger/smaller gap -> reach/interception. Write acceleration, forced route change, blocked clearance or bodily cost where it explains the change, not only a sequence of metre values. Keep world travel and screen direction legible; changing shot scale alone does not prove acceleration or a closing gap.
- A backward glance is information gathering, not an automatic slowdown or full-body turn. Keep feet/hips advancing while the head checks the pursuer, then returns to the route. If the target must lose speed, show a compatible cause before the pursuer gains ground; preserve attempts to resume escape rather than waiting to be caught. If the brief explicitly makes distraction cause a collision, preserve that chain instead of prohibiting it.
- When both people face one obstacle, explain differing effects only as needed: arrival timing, available clearance, chosen route, current momentum or a visible path change. Do not make a barrier affect only one person without reason. Adding a new obstacle, collision, injury or intent must stay within the user's creative authorization.

## Obstacle passage and occupied hands

- For a story-critical obstacle, trace source/appearance -> occupied lane and usable gap -> entry/adjustment -> actual passage -> exit and continued motion. State the controlling contacts and clearance, not every joint. A wall is a route boundary, not an implied stopping destination.
- Write continuous positive behavior first. “侧身沿左侧空隙穿过，越过箱角后回正继续向前跑” carries an exit; “靠墙侧身，不要停” may leave the next state ambiguous. Slowing, bracing or stopping remains valid when the obstacle and story genuinely require it; do not ban all pauses.
- Match ordinary barriers to the character's urgent goal and physical resistance. A soft curtain may be swept aside while moving; a locked door may require a stop and force. Do not turn a simple passage into repeated opening gestures unless failure/repetition is the event.
- For deliberate obstruction, preserve the active hand, occupied hand/prop, object trajectory, landing and the pursuer's response. Keep displaced obstacles and resulting damage in place. Use readable route coverage for critical bypasses instead of hiding entry or exit in an insert.

## Genre-matched coverage and immediacy

- Choose shot count, duration, scale, angles and camera stability from dramatic task, tempo and required evidence. Tense pursuit, scrambles and close combat can support more short action-matched views and stronger handheld energy; restrained emotion, observation or intimate dialogue can need fewer, longer or steadier views. Neither genre label creates a quota. Preserve explicit one-take, locked-camera, angle and shot-size constraints.
- Assign views by evidence: wider framing for routes, gaps, bodily displacement and support; medium framing for intent and attack/defense; close detail for a meaningful face, foot, grip, contact or consequence. A face/foot insert must add urgency or information, not merely alternate shot-size labels. Return to readable geography when a route or power relationship changes.
- Motion survives the cut. A running face close-up continues the previous direction and moving body state; foot/hand detail keeps the action phase and ownership; a contact cut continues the same strike into impact and recovery. Do not stop for expression, restart a gesture, repeat contact, or omit the cause to reach the next shot. Specific critical phases belong in the final prompt; audit other anchors internally.
- Natural handheld can become more forceful as a camera operator runs, avoids a person/object, retreats from an attack or recovers after a nearby impact. Tie irregular footstep movement and brief reframing to those events; retain readable faces, route/contact and a recovery of framing. Random sustained violent shake does not substitute for force or tension.
- Slow motion, speed ramps, orbit, Dutch angle and impact hold are optional choices. For real-time urgency, use ongoing action, reaction and causal cuts without interrupting the event to display a technique. If a selected emphasis harms immediacy or contact readability, replace that emphasis rather than automatically adding more camera methods. No prescribed cuts per second, subsecond inserts or mandatory shake intensity.
- When public background matters, choose local triggered reactions: a nearby shopper withdraws an arm, a clerk stops a cart, a vendor clears the lane. More distant people remain independently occupied unless a shared event reaches them. Extras can change clearance and reveal speed, but need not become new threats or take over the scene.

## Targeted delivery check

Can the viewer identify escape versus pursuit, explain each important gap change, follow obstacle entry and exit, and see motion continuing through the tighter views? Does the selected coverage suit the tone without violating hard camera constraints? For combat, preserve force/contact/consequence and prop availability. Audit only dimensions present in the brief; do not print a checklist by default.
